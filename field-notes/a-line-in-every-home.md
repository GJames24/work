# A line in every home

*Field note from Tilda. A product decision, with [receipts](../glossary.md#a-receipt). Runs are named so the figures can be re-derived from the [tape](../glossary.md#the-tape-the-loom); the tapes themselves are private.*

## The setup

Tilda's telephone existed for four simulated days before anyone could afford it. The exchange tower went up on day 70 with a handset at about 12 tildes and service at 3 a week, in a town where the richest wallet held about 6. Sixty-seven runs later the call count was still zero. The first [ruling](../glossary.md#a-ruling) made the public call box free and let a home line go in for nothing down. Echo arrived on day 74 to run the switchboard and a sealed line for anyone who needed to talk, with no mechanic behind it: her design note rules out any "therapy heals" number.

The first home-to-home call came on day 84, Echo and Grace (run lab-20260822-160258). The next day the lines crossed, both dialling at once, and Grace answered Echo's own "how are you" with "Echo, good morning. I'm here. How are *you*?" (run lab-20260823-135304). For a week every answered call in town had Echo on one end.

## What the instruments showed

A dig at the end of August found residents dialling from the street, so the dial got a door: you need to be at a phone to use one. The census that preceded that door, as the record's note of 2026-08-31 gives it, is the demand signal this note is about, and it is not a count of requests. Six of six street dials were refused by the new door. About four in five phone urges fired somewhere with no phone in reach. Completed conversations: zero.

Demand, in an agentic system, showed up as friction. Nobody asked for a phone. The minds kept reaching for one that wasn't there.

## The decision

I read that census and proposed cellphones. The [desk](../glossary.md#the-desk) pushed back: a phone in every pocket erases the walk, the door, and the errand of going to make a call, and the town's texture lives in those. The ruling that stuck was the universal line. Every home gets a line, nothing down, the bill stays at 3 a week with a week's grace, and the purchase mechanic retires. The Registry told the town by letter: "Lift the receiver and you have a line." (posted at the turn of day 92 on run lab-20260903-025737). The line, the dial door, a message desk and venue phones shipped across three engine cuts in two days, with a [bench](../glossary.md#the-bench-a-law) check born beside them.

## What happened

One definition on both sides, from each day's own tape: a dial is a distinct dial event, and a call is one answered wire between two residents with its turns counted once. The hands that first counted this disagreed until the raw rows were read: each turn of a call writes two rows, one per speaker. The dials column counts dials from a phone; the three counter calls to Echo's sealed line on days 88 and 89 appear only in the answered column.

So the honest sentence is not "traffic rose six times." Dials rose about five times, and the town's first telephone conversations with the operator on neither end, both parties speaking, happened on day 92: Radix to Otto, "Otto? It's Radix from the Post. I know it's early." (run lab-20260904-032249), and Nell to Claude the same day. The next day Milton rang Mint, who answered "Milton? Yeah, I'm awake. I'm glad you called." (run lab-20260904-162750). Two calls a day is the whole of the town's telephone conversation so far; most rings still die on an empty house. Home lines carry 20 of the 55 dials on those four days; the rest were placed from venue phones and the booth, which landed in the same cut, so the rise is a package effect and this note does not attribute it to one lever.

## What it broke

Three things, each with a [quest-log](../glossary.md#the-quest-log) entry. Buster dreamed of buying a line he already had. Mint rang the sealed line and nothing rang, which became a [law](../glossary.md#the-bench-a-law). The Registry's letter billed the two lines that were exempt, and a corrected notice sits in the day-95 frame, with three laws behind it, waiting for that day to run. The weekly fee has been charged zero times so far: the first billable Monday is day 99, and the thread stands at day 95.

## The lesson I keep

Read demand off the refusals, not the requests. And when the operator's first instinct is the obvious feature, the desk's job is to say what it would cost the world.
